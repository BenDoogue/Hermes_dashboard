1. The Scale of the Cosmos (1/26th of a Degree)

In December 1995, the Hubble Space Telescope stared at an apparently empty patch of sky in the constellation Ursa Major (near the Big Dipper) for 10 consecutive days. That tiny patch covered an angular area of just 2.4 arcminutes on a side—roughly 1/26th of an angular degree.

Full Moon

~30 arcminutes across (0.5°). The Hubble Deep Field patch fits inside the Moon's disk over 100 times!

Grain of Sand at Arm's Length

Spans about 1 arcminute. The Deep Field patch is comparable to holding a grain of sand at arm's length.

Outstretched Finger

Covers roughly 1 degree of the night sky—making the Deep Field ~26 times smaller than your fingertip!
